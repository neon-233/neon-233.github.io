This guide introduces our five research directions through papers coauthored by Yujin Ji, helping undergraduates understand the questions, foundational knowledge, and methods behind our work.

Structural Evolution

ATOMIC STRUCTURE & DYNAMICS

How do atoms in materials rearrange themselves?

A material is not a static structural picture. During synthesis, heating, or use, atoms move, defects emerge, and a crystal may adopt a different arrangement. These changes affect stability, catalytic activity, and heat transport. This direction asks how structures form, why they change, and what those changes do . The work draws on structural chemistry, thermodynamics, and statistical physics. We first use density functional theory (DFT) to calculate electronic structures, energies, and atomic forces, then use molecular dynamics (MD) to follow atomic motion and compare the results with diffraction, microscopy, and other experiments. The iridium-oxide nanoribbon study shown here illustrates how precursors can evolve into a metastable crystalline phase. [1]

structural chemistry thermodynamics DFT/Molecular Dynamics

How AI Can Help

Machine-learning potentials learn energies and forces from quantum calculations, making larger systems and longer dynamical simulations accessible. In a CsPbBr3 study coauthored by Yujin Ji, deep-potential molecular dynamics was used to investigate how cesium vacancies affect heat transport. Reliability in new structures still requires validation. [5]

02

Surface Reaction Mechanisms

SURFACE REACTION MECHANISMS

What steps does a chemical reaction go through?

Energy processes such as water electrolysis and fuel-cell reactions often occur on catalyst surfaces. A seemingly simple overall reaction involves molecular adsorption, bond breaking and formation, and product desorption. This research seeks to identify the active sites, possible reaction pathways, and steps that limit efficiency . A foundation in physical chemistry, quantum chemistry, and electrochemistry is useful. We typically use DFT to compare adsorption structures and reaction free energies, search for transition states and calculate activation barriers where necessary, and consider solvent and electrode-potential effects. For example, the study of single tungsten atoms in nickel hydroxide explains changes in oxygen-evolution performance by comparing individual reaction steps. [2]

How AI Can Help

AI can estimate adsorption energies and screen active sites, directing expensive quantum calculations toward promising pathways. Its predictions still need to be checked with physical models. The figure in this section presents reaction-mechanism analysis, rather than output from an AI model.

Structure–Property Relationships

STRUCTURE–PROPERTY RELATIONSHIPS

Why does the performance change if only a few atoms are replaced?

Even with similar chemical compositions, different atomic arrangements, dopant positions, and local coordination can lead to very different conductivity, stability, or catalytic behavior. Structure–property research connects what changed in the structure with why the properties changed , seeking principles that can guide materials design. This requires structural chemistry, quantum mechanics, and data analysis. We compare band structures, densities of states, and charge distributions using DFT, then combine these results with adsorption and reaction calculations to identify the structural features that control performance. For phosphorus-doped MoS2, for example, we can vary the number and positions of phosphorus atoms, follow the resulting electronic changes, and analyze their influence on oxygen-reduction activity. [3]

electronic structure Doping and local coordination Descriptors/Data Analysis

How AI Can Help

Machine learning can identify correlations in large structure–property datasets and help prioritize which features to investigate. Feature importance does not establish a causal mechanism. Controlled calculations that change one structural factor at a time are needed to test an explanation.

04

High-throughput Materials Screening

HIGH-THROUGHPUT MATERIALS SCREENING

There are thousands of candidates, which one should be studied first?

Varying elemental composition, crystal structure, and doping strategy rapidly expands the number of candidate materials. High-throughput screening uses consistent, reproducible workflows to identify candidates that merit further study . Alongside materials science and quantum chemistry, the work uses Python, Linux, automated task management, and statistical analysis: building structures in batches, calculating stability and target properties, and screening against explicit criteria. In the CrystalGNN study coauthored by Yujin Ji, crystals are represented as graphs of atoms and their relationships. The model learns patterns in DFT data and accelerates the search for metastable IrO2 structures. [4]

Python/Automation Materials Databases Graph Neural Networks

How AI Can Help

Graph neural networks can quickly estimate candidate properties, narrow the search space, and help select structures for more accurate calculations. Errors must be assessed on independent test data, with particular attention to reliability for elements or structures outside the training distribution.

05

Materials Design & Optimization

MATERIALS DESIGN & OPTIMIZATION

Can we design the structure from the target performance in reverse?

Understanding these relationships also allows us to propose new structures: changing crystal phases, dopants, strain, or interfaces to seek better catalysts and energy materials. Starting from the target properties, this research aims to balance activity, stability, and synthetic feasibility . We combine chemical intuition, crystallography, thermodynamics, and optimization, repeatedly evaluating candidates through structure searches, accurate DFT calculations, and surface-reaction analysis. CrystalGNN identified new metastable structures among many IrO2 configurations and evaluated their oxygen-evolution potential, illustrating a propose–predict–validate design process. Computational discoveries still require experimental testing. [4]

Crystal Structure Search multi-objective optimization Comparison of theory and experiment

How AI Can Help

Beyond graph-network screening, active learning can select the most informative next calculations, while generative models explore ways to propose new structures. Structure generation is an emerging research direction: a generated candidate must not be presented as an already synthesized or validated material. [6]

AI × COMPUTATIONAL CHEMISTRY

Extending what we can compute,
with evidence behind every prediction.

Artificial intelligence is expanding the tools of computational chemistry and materials science: learning structure–property relationships, modeling interatomic interactions, and proposing candidate structures. The following three studies, coauthored by Yujin Ji, illustrate these developments.

GRAPH NEURAL NETWORKS / 2023

Learning Properties from Structure

CrystalGNN constructs features directly from atoms and their geometric relationships, reducing reliance on manually chosen descriptors. The study used it to predict the energies of many IrO2 configurations and identify promising metastable phases for further calculation. [4]

DEEP POTENTIAL / 2025

Extending Atomistic Simulations

Deep-potential models learn energies and atomic forces from DFT data, reducing the cost of each step of a molecular-dynamics simulation. In the CsPbBr3 study, this approach was used to investigate relationships between vacancies, lattice distortions, and heat transport. [5]

TRANSFORMERS / PERSPECTIVE

From Prediction to Structure Generation

The review discusses progress and prospects for Transformers in chemical-property prediction, structure generation, and related tasks. These tools expand data-driven research while requiring careful evaluation of data quality, applicability, and physical plausibility. [6]

A foundation in chemistry and physics helps you ask meaningful questions; programming and machine learning help you investigate them efficiently. Undergraduates should develop both sets of skills together.

A SMALL READING GUIDE

You do not need to know every term
to begin with a question.

Choose a direction that interests you, understand the question it addresses, and then explore the figures and paper abstracts. Physical chemistry, structural chemistry, and basic data processing in Python provide a useful foundation for getting started.

DFT · density functional theory

A widely used quantum-mechanical method that starts from electron density to calculate a system's energy and electronic structure. It can compare structural stability, calculate forces, and analyze reactions, but results depend on the chosen model and approximations.

MD · Molecular Dynamics

Molecular dynamics computes atomic motion step by step from interatomic interactions. It lets us investigate structural changes and transport at finite temperatures. The accessible system size and simulation duration depend on the model and computational cost.

GNN · Graph Neural Network

A graph neural network treats atoms as nodes and relationships such as neighboring connections as edges. Information passes through the graph to learn structural features. This representation suits molecules and crystals and is often used to predict energies or other material properties.

What is the difference between reaction free energy and activation energy barrier?

Reaction free energy compares the thermodynamic states at the beginning and end of a step. An activation barrier describes the energy needed to cross a transition state. The former indicates thermodynamic driving force; the latter is related to reaction rate. They are different quantities.
